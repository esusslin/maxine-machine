How picks are made

Every pick runs the same eight steps, once a day at 2:00 PM Pacific for that day's games, or from Admin → Run full formula.

1 · Data

Every NHL regular-season game since 2021-22 from the NHL's own API: score, how it ended (regulation, OT, shootout), goalies, and every shot with its location. Refreshed before each run.

2 · Expected goals (xG) for every shot

The chance an unblocked shot becomes a goal, from distance and angle to the net, shot type, rebound (same team shot within 3 s), rush (other team had the puck within 4 s), skater advantage, third period and overtime. Logistic regression; each season scored by a model fit only on earlier seasons. Blocked shots, shootouts and empty-net shots excluded.

3 · Team form before each game

Exponentially weighted averages with a 15-game half-life, carried across seasons (October leans on last year; Utah continues Arizona): xG for and against per game, goals for and against per game, 5-on-5 xG share. Rest: days since the last game (capped at 4) and a back-to-back flag for each team.

3b · Expected starting goalie

At bet time the starter is usually not announced, so each team's goalie input is a probability-weighted average of its possible starters. Candidates: every goalie who started one of the team's last 20 games. Chance each one starts: logistic regression on his share of the last 5, 10 and 20 starts, whether he started the previous game, whether the team is on a back-to-back (the goalie who played last night usually sits), and days since his last start; the chances add up to 100%. Each goalie's quality: career goals saved above expected per 30 shots, shrunk toward league average (3000 pseudo-shots, so a goalie with little history counts as average). The most likely starter is right about two times in three; the board shows him with his chance.

4a · Moneyline model (who wins, OT and shootout included)

Logistic regression on home-minus-away differences: xG differential, goal differential, 5v5 xG share, expected goalie quality, home back-to-back, away back-to-back, rest difference. The intercept is home ice. Then calibrated: p → sigmoid(a + b·logit p), fit on the model's own out-of-sample predictions, which pulls back overconfident calls.

4b · Goal model (totals, puck line, regulation)

Each team's regulation goals ~ Poisson(λ), with λ from a Poisson regression on its own xG and goals for, the opponent's xG and goals against, the opponent's expected goalie quality, home ice, back-to-backs and rest. The goalie decides who scores, not how many: the two λs are rescaled so their sum matches the same model without goalies (adding goalies to the total made totals slightly worse). From the two λs, a full score grid (0-12 goals each), then three corrections fit on past seasons:

  • Regulation ties are more common than independent goals predict: the tie cells are scaled up.
  • Empty-net goals: a share of one-goal wins become two-goal wins because the trailing team pulls its goalie.
  • Overtime/shootout: who wins it leans toward the stronger team, P = logistic(a + b·log(λhome/λaway)). Its winner is credited one goal, as books grade totals and puck lines.

Totals and puck line probabilities are calibrated again per market on past results.

5 · Market price

One live odds snapshot (US books: moneyline, totals, puck line; EU books: regulation 3-way with a Draw). Each book's margin is removed (p = (1/price) ÷ sum of 1/price over its outcomes), then the median across books is the market's probability. The line used is the one most books quote. The best price and its book are recorded.

6 · Edge

For each market, the side the model likes more than the market does. Edge = model probability − market probability for that side (in regulation 3-way: the outcome with the largest positive gap).

7 · What becomes a pick

  • Edge at least: .
  • Clean: neither team plays another game first, so the model has every result the bet depends on.
  • A line is posted and the game has not started.
  • Today's games only, priced at 2:00 PM Pacific against the odds at that moment.

7b · One pick per game per team

Moneyline, puck line and regulation on the same team win or lose together, so only the one with the largest edge is picked; the others show "same bet as". Totals and the regulation tie are separate. Backtest 2023-26: 1,171 picks instead of 1,626, value vs the fair closing price +1.3% → +1.6%, worst losing stretch 33 → 24.

8 · Tracking

The bet is the first price seen. About 15-30 minutes before puck drop the closing price is captured for the same market and line; beating it (CLV) is the early sign the edge is real. Graded at 3:30 the next morning: final score for moneyline, totals and puck line; regulation goals only for the 3-way.
